New Plymouth: Rainforest Dreams and Misty Wonderlands

Preface#

Since we hadn’t traveled far in quite a while, at the end of February, my girlfriend Chelsea, our friend Jennie, and I planned a trip to New Plymouth on New Zealand’s North Island.

I first noticed this place because of its beautiful snow-capped mountain. This time, our goal was to hike and see the mountain up close. If you check out this area on Google Maps Street View, you’ll notice a perfect circle of greenery — right at the center of that circle is our destination: Mt. Taranaki, often called the “Mt. Fuji of New Zealand.”

Day 1#

We set off from Auckland in the morning, picked up Jennie, and started driving south along State Highway 1 — a journey that took about five hours in total. Chelsea took the first stretch from Auckland to Hamilton, getting some experience with long-distance driving. I took over for the rest of the way.

On the drive, we passed many charming small towns and vast farmlands, with rolling hills filled with sheep and cows, and tall, lush cornfields lining the roads.

We arrived in New Plymouth around 2 p.m. and had a tasty Vietnamese lunch. Our first stop was Cape Egmont Lighthouse. This white lighthouse stands between the green pastures and the rugged coastline, with the cloud-covered peak of Taranaki looming in the background. It felt like a silent guardian watching over the land. There were no crowds, just the sound of the wind and waves. The sunlight filtered through the clouds and lit up the lighthouse — as if time itself had slowed.

Back in town, we visited New Plymouth’s famous Coastal Walkway. We stopped at the well-known seaside railway crossing (like a mini version of Kamakura in Japan), then followed the walkway to the iconic Wind Wand — a red, bendy art installation dancing in the sea breeze. It represents the free, flowing, and artistic soul of the city.

Dinner was at a popular Thai restaurant. Unexpectedly, a group of elderly locals from a nearby retirement home were also dining there. After eating, they gathered together and sang a beautiful Māori song. It was the first full Māori-language song I’d heard since moving to New Zealand two years ago. Though I didn’t understand the lyrics, the harmonies and shared emotion moved me. One of the elders even came up afterward and asked why we hadn’t sung along — I told him I really wanted to, but sadly, I didn’t know the words.

Day 2#

We started the day with breakfast at Elixir Café, a great spot with delicious brunch options — highly recommended! Then we headed out to Kent Road to find the famous photo spot with the road leading directly to Mt. Taranaki. Sadly, we struggled to find it at first and only located it after doubling back. Even then, the mountain was hidden behind clouds. We still didn’t realize how bad the visibility would get later…

Next up was the highlight of the trip: hiking around Mt. Taranaki. One famous photo spot on this trail features the mountain perfectly reflected in a small alpine pool. The trail itself is relatively flat but long — about 15km return and roughly 6 hours to complete.

After parking and a quick toilet break, we took a group photo and started the hike. Most of the trail winds through native forest, so it’s shaded and not too hot. The path is well maintained, flat, and easygoing — a truly pleasant walk.

Unfortunately, Jennie isn’t a regular hiker, and halfway through the hike, she was exhausted. We kept encouraging her, saying “Just around the next corner!” to keep her going — and to her credit, it worked. When we reached the ridge, light rain started falling. By the time we arrived at the reflection point, the entire area was engulfed in thick fog. Visibility dropped to less than 20 meters. We could barely see the water, let alone the mountain or its reflection. We asked two other hikers if they’d seen the mountain — they laughed and said, “Maybe it’s out there somewhere.”

We had a snack and began the return journey. Jennie, claiming her legs were “gone,” hobbled her way down. As we descended, the rain intensified. We went from light drizzle to steady rain, arriving back at the car drenched but laughing — a perfect “before and after” photo moment.

Back at the carpark, we chatted with a couple of hikers who were planning to stay overnight and return early for sunrise photos. We wished them the best of luck!

Exhausted, we headed back to our Airbnb to rest.

Day 3#

The weather was finally clear, and Mt. Taranaki stood tall and proud — visible even from the road. We regretted not having this weather the day before, but made the most of it by heading to Te Rewa Rewa Bridge for some final mountain photos. It was our last planned day in New Plymouth, so we hit the road for our next destination: Skyline Rotorua, to ride the luge. Chelsea remembered it fondly from last time, and we thought it’d be a fun way to wrap up the trip. On the drive, Chelsea said three days wasn’t enough — so we decided to extend the trip by one more day.

But when we arrived at Skyline, the gondola was broken. With no estimate for when it would be fixed, we changed plans and went to soak at Polynesian Spa, Rotorua’s most famous geothermal hot spring. The ticket we bought gave us access to seven pools.

As we neared Rotorua, the smell of sulfur filled the air — the famous “rotten egg” aroma that comes with geothermal activity. At the spa, the scent was even stronger, but the warm pools made it all worth it.

For dinner, we went to Yamato Japanese Restaurant, where you sit around the kitchen and watch chefs prepare everything fresh — sashimi, sushi, and grilled meats. Everything was delicious and the experience was intimate and lively.

Day 4#

This spontaneous extra day turned out to be a blast. We started the morning with breakfast from Rotorua’s most popular bakery, then headed to Agrodome Farm.

There, we watched a sheep show, hand-fed alpacas and sheep, tasted local honey, admired kiwi vines, and even saw a few deer!

Final Thoughts#

Four days and three nights — a trip filled with laughter, surprises, and unforgettable moments. Despite the rain, fog, and a broken gondola, we came away with so many great memories. From the elderly choir singing in Māori to alpacas chasing us for food, every moment was worth it.

Can’t wait for the next adventure.
